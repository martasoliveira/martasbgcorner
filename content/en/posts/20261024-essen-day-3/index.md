Hello everyone!

Here we go with Day 3, the last day of my very first SPIEL Essen! If you missed the start of the adventure, you can catch up on [Day 1](https://www.martasbgcorner.com/en/posts/20261022-essen-day-1/) and [Day 2](https://www.martasbgcorner.com/en/posts/20261023-essen-day-2/).

### Going solo

The sky was grey today, but at least it didn't rain. It was cold, though!

My boyfriend wasn't feeling well, so I went to the Messe alone. It was a different experience, with its positives and its negatives. I missed having my partner by my side to share my findings and my enthusiasm. On the other hand, without him to talk to, I ended up moving around more and talking to a lot more people.

### Synapses Games: Chimerium

*Designed by Eric Dubus and Olivier Melison · Illustrated by Alexandre Bonvalot · Published by Synapses Games · [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/462396/chimerium)*

My first stop was Chimerium. The magical universe and how beautiful the game looks on the table made me stop and take a careful look.

One of the presenters gave me a quick explanation. You draft dice to study new species and acquire powerful artefacts for your laboratory, then manage your resources to create unique Chimeras and trigger chain reactions.

Of course, I asked about the solo mode. You play against an opponent, and that was enough to put me under its spell. The game isn't on sale yet, so I'll be keeping a close eye on it.

### inPatience: Karnivorous, Mazing and Mayor of Chicago

*Karnivorous: designed by Inka and Markus Brand · Illustrated by Vincent Dutrait · [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/413255/karnivorous)*
*Mazing: designed by Jim Deacove · [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/438173/mazing)*
*Mayor of Chicago: designed by Xavier Georges · [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/413252/mayor-of-chicago)*
*Published by inPatience*

I'm a big fan of inPatience games, so this booth was a must, and I spent my morning there. Sofiane presented three games to me, and he was awesome. He told me he hasn't been working at the company for very long, but he's doing a wonderful job.

{{< figure
  src="essen-3-inpatience-booth.jpg"
  alt="Marta and Sofiane smiling together at the inPatience booth"

  caption="With Sofiane at the inPatience booth"

**Karnivorous** made me fall in love straight away. First, the theme, with all its humour: scientists trying to end world hunger with giant apples used their discovery on the wrong plants, and now the plants want to eat everyone! The only ones left to stop them are brave gardener-robots.

Then there's that puzzle-like management typical of inPatience games. Sofiane explained that the plants spread whether you complete the quests or not, but they spread less if you do. Completing all the quests every round is extremely hard, almost impossible. So you have to make choices and sacrifice things carefully. I love that side of it, and the puzzle side too. The colours are lovely as well. It's a cooperative game for 1 to 4 players, and it came home with me!

{{< figure
  src="essen-3-karnivorous.jpg"
  alt="The Karnivorous lab board, with its rooms filled with plants and robots"
  width="400"
  caption="Karnivorous: a lab overrun by hungry plants"
>}}

Next, I played **Mazing**, an abstract game where two white and two black pieces must swap sides across the board. Some pieces can only travel on white spaces, others only on black ones, each with its own movement, and some spaces block a piece for good.

For my first two moves, I was completely lost and thinking, "this is impossible!" Then Sofiane suggested moving the black and white pieces that travel through the white spaces first, and the ones that travel through the black spaces afterwards, as it makes it easier to trace a path. Suddenly it all made sense, and I managed to win! I'm not entirely sure I didn't cheat somewhere, but it was so much fun, and the ending was pure joy.

{{< figure
  src="essen-3-mazing.jpg"
  alt="The Mazing board, a black and white maze with the pieces in place"

  caption="Mazing: impossible at first, pure joy at the end"

Then came **Mayor of Chicago**, a solo game. Sofiane explained it and stayed to watch my game, getting excited right along with me. I had built a perfect engine and could easily have won, but I miscounted my cards and lost because of one miserable card. Sofiane shared my frustration, and honestly, that made the moment just perfect. I didn't buy it this time, but it's already on my shopping list for the near future.

### A signature from Owen Davey

*Fame & Fable: designed and illustrated by Owen Davey · Published by Board Game Circus · [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/421254/fame-and-fable)*

After lunch, I spent the afternoon moving from booth to booth, discovering new games.

Second attempt at Fame & Fable! I still didn't get to learn the mechanics, as Owen Davey was in the middle of a signing session. But I did leave with a signature from the designer and illustrator himself! Discovering how it plays is now on my to-do list.

### ICE Makes: Age of Legend

*Designed by Jeffrey CCH · Published by ICE Makes · [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/479637/age-of-legend)*

At ICE Makes, I was presented with Age of Legend, which is coming to Gamefound. It comes in a small box, similar to [Age of Civilization](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/264647/age-of-civilization). I liked the art, and I loved how each card can be used in different ways: for its power, placed above the board, or for its resources, placed at the bottom. A small box, but a big promise. I'll be watching that campaign!

### A familiar face

In the middle of the crowd, I bumped into some friends from Legião dos Jogos, my board game group in Braga, and we stopped for a little chat. Even in a fair this huge, you can still find people from home!

### Kinson Key Games: Excursions

*Designed by Ben Rosset · Illustrated by Ian O'Toole · Published by Kinson Key Games · [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/468800/excursions)*

I stopped at Kinson Key Games to understand the difference between [Galactic Cruise](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/391137/galactic-cruise), which I own, and their new game, Excursions. Excursions comes with a lovely circular board, and it's smaller and easier than Galactic Cruise.

But after the presentation, I couldn't really find enough differences between the two to make me back it. In my opinion, it overlaps a bit too much with Galactic Cruise. That said, I didn't play it, so this is based on the presentation only.

### Thistroy Games: Micro Architects

*Designed by Michał Jagodziński and Kamil Langie · Illustrated by Jarosław Wajs · Published by [Thistroy Games](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgamepublisher/36458/thistroy-games) · [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/374199/micro-architects)*

At Thistroy Games, a lady presented Micro Architects and its expansion, Big City Vacation, to me. The game is lovely! I liked the mechanic of selecting buildings to construct your city and meet objectives. It's beautiful, even though the theme doesn't leave much room for big illustrations. What I liked most is that it comes in a small box, so it's easy to carry around.

### KYF Edition: Behind

*Designed by Cédric Millet · Published by KYF Edition · [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/423787/behind)*

Behind had been on my wishlist for so long that I was really happy to finally get the chance to test it! I played the convention scenario alone, and I managed to solve it.

It was so much fun to put the pieces together like a puzzle while following the clues in the design. The game made a lot of sense, and building the solution was a real pleasure.

I'm deliberately not sharing a photo, so I don't spoil anything for those of you who might be at the Messe tomorrow and want to try it!

### Quick looks

A few more games crossed my path today, more briefly:

- **[Skirmish: Battle for Draconia](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/429580/skirmish-battle-for-draconia)** (Jeffrey CCH, ICE Makes): the card design is what made me stop. It's a card game for 2 to 4 players where you battle your enemies, and where you place each card matters, because it fights the card in the same place on your opponent's side. I only saw it presented.
- **[Timespan](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/462479/timespan)** (Kristian Amundsen Østby and Kenneth Minde, Alion – by Dr Ø): another one from my wishlist. I only took a quick look, and it seemed crowded on the table, but maybe that's because I saw a 5-player game. I'd like to dig a little deeper into this one.
- **[Fear of the Dark](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/427439/fear-of-the-dark)** (InterHuman): I went back, as promised on Day 2, and realised it's a dungeon crawler. The art didn't attract me that much in person, so I didn't ask for an explanation. The feeling just wasn't there.

### Lucky Duck Games: Accidental Heroes and Oakspire

*Accidental Heroes: designed by Wojciech Grajkowski · [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/474322/accidental-heroes)*
*Oakspire: [BGG page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/463976/oakspire-the-builders-of-the-sunleaf-grove)*
*Published by Lucky Duck Games*

Last but not least, I stopped at Lucky Duck Games, where Ben presented their crowdfunding projects.

**Accidental Heroes** launches on Kickstarter on 27 October. It's a digital hybrid adventure board game with a companion app. To be honest, when Ben mentioned the app, I froze a little, because I prefer games without app dependency, even though I own some that have it. Ben explained that most of the game happens outside the app, but it's still hard for me.

And then there was **Oakspire**, which made me fall in love as soon as I looked at it. The game is gorgeous: the illustrations of cozy animals are simply beautiful, and the boards are lovely too. It's a tableau building game where each card can be used in multiple ways. In solo, you play to complete challenges from the back of the rulebook. I haven't played it yet, but what I saw and what Ben told me was enough: I'll be following the project, and I definitely want to play it.

### My favourites of the day

The inPatience booth was my favourite moment. I felt at home there, like playing with a friend while discovering exciting new games.

And for the eyes, Oakspire and Chimerium win the day: they both conquered me with how beautiful they look.

### Goodbye, SPIEL Essen

And that's a wrap on my very first SPIEL Essen!

Being on my own today, I managed to see more games than on the other days. And since I was alone, I really had no excuses: I had to be the one asking for explanations, so there was no room to be shy. I loved the experience.

  src="essen-3-goodbye-essen.jpg"

Over three days, four games came home with me: Chopstick Tightrope, Light in the Dark, Gloomhaven: Buttons & Bugs and Karnivorous.

Will I go back? For sure! If you're into board games, it's the place to be.
