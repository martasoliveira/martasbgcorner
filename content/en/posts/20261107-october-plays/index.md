## Favourite Game of the Month

### Moustache

*Designed by Alexandre Aguilar and Jules Messaud, published by KODAMA.*

Moustache was my top game of Essen and my favourite of the whole month. I completely fell in love with it! It's a game for 3 to 6 players, so not my usual kind of game, but it's so much fun with the right people. I got to try it at Essen with two wonderful players who made the game shine, and we had the time of our lives.

It's a competitive trick-taking game where each round adds a new rule and you team up with a random player. You're trying to earn the most points by winning tricks and taking trophies, each with a different value. The added rules are fantastic: a type of card you really don't want in the early rounds can become the one everyone envies in the last round. In our game, the stars on green cards counted negatively for the first three rounds. Then in round four, a new rule made the team with the fewest stars win the round, the complete opposite, so those green cards became pure gold! It was so funny that we still talk about it today. Thank you, Vanessa and Leticia, for that moment.

Moustache gets a 9/10 from me.

## My SPIEL Essen Haul

I didn't lose my head at Essen, and five games came home with me: Karnivorous, Light in the Dark, Gloomhaven: Buttons & Bugs, Chopstick Tightrope and Cat Days. I've already played four of them this month. Only Cat Days hasn't hit the table yet.

### Karnivorous

*Designed by Inka and Markus Brand, published by inPatience.*

The story behind Karnivorous is almost funny. A group of scientists is developing a genetic modification to make apple trees grow faster and bigger, so they produce more and bigger apples. But... they apply it to the wrong plant and trigger a plant apocalypse! And guess who they're counting on to save them, the lab and the world? The helper robots.

{{< figure
  src="october-plays-karnivorous.jpg"
  alt="Karnivorous in progress, with a lab board covered in green and orange plant tokens, white robot pieces, a dice action board and alarm cards"
  width="500"
  caption="A lab overrun by plants, with my robots trying to keep up"

It's a puzzle-style game where you always seem to be short of actions. You roll dice that help you decide your actions, then solve objectives, but you have to plan carefully which ones you want to tackle. It's entertaining and a lot of fun. I played it twice, losing once and winning once. I want a few more plays before writing the full review, but it already earns an 8.5/10.

### Chopstick Tightrope

*Published by Gorilla Games.*

This is a party game, and I have to say I love it. I'm not that agile with chopsticks, since I only learned to use them recently, but the game makes me want to improve. All three of my plays were at two players with my boyfriend, which is rare, and that made it even more special. It's a 9/10 for me, and a full review is coming next week!

### Light in the Dark

*Designed by Kai Starck, published by Archona Games.*

In Light in the Dark, you're a plague doctor curing villages and fighting the bad guys. It's a dungeon crawler with a lot of potential. I've only played once (and won!), but I really appreciated the mechanics, especially how the enemies and the doctor move. I'll need more plays to judge how replayable it is, but so far I'm happy with the game and its theme. Because of those doubts, I'm giving it 7/10 for now, and I'm looking forward to the Fires of Thokaia expansion, which I think will improve the game a lot.

### Gloomhaven: Buttons & Bugs

*Designed by Joe Klipfel and Nikki Valens, published by Cephalofair Games.*

This one isn't new, but I'd always been reluctant to try the big Gloomhaven. I'd watched some videos and wasn't convinced at all. So I went to talk to the person at the booth to find out more. The presentation was really good and won me over, and the tiny box fitted in my luggage (otherwise I'd have had to ship it home).

I played it once in October and already twice in November, and so far I'm enjoying it, though I've realised I'm not very good at it: I already lost on scenario 2. Finishing the campaign is going to be a struggle! What I like most is how quick it is. Both setup and play are fast, so it's perfect for a little break or the end of a tiring day. On the downside, the small size is great for carrying it around, but it makes everything hard to read and handle. It's cute, but not that practical. It's a 7/10 at this stage, and I'm sure I'll enjoy the gameplay more once I get the hang of it.

## Played at SPIEL Essen

Now let's talk about the games I played at Essen but didn't buy (at least, not yet!).

### Mayor of Chicago

*Designed by Xavier Georges, published by inPatience.*

I have to confess that once I got home, I pre-ordered it from my usual shop, and now I'm waiting for it to arrive. That says a lot about how much I enjoyed it! Like most inPatience games, it's a solo puzzle game. You're competing against another candidate to become mayor of Chicago, and you need the most votes at the end of the game. You place cards to build the city and bring people in, earning both immediate and permanent bonuses. But be careful: your deck is limited, and each time you complete a column, you need to meet certain conditions to earn more benefits, or bad things happen.

{{< figure
  src="october-plays-mayor-chicago.jpg"
  alt="Mayor of Chicago in progress on a demo table, with a grid of city cards, animal assistant cards and a row of newspaper event cards"
  width="400"
  caption="Building my city at the inPatience booth, one headline at a time"

You build an engine to earn more points and votes, which I really enjoyed. I loved the artwork and the theme: it's fun and clever. And yes, I lost, because of a bad move right near the end... I miscounted my cards! It's an 8/10 so far, and I can't wait to see how it holds up once my copy arrives.

### More Essen Discoveries

**Mazing** (*designed by Jim Deacove, published by inPatience*): I played it solo at the inPatience booth. It's an abstract game where you try to bring the black main pieces to one side of the board and the white ones to the opposite side. Each piece has its own movement rule, and some spaces make you flip a piece face down, leaving it stuck. It's almost like chess, but you can play it solo. I had a lot of fun! I don't think I'll buy it, but I'd happily play it again. I'm giving it 7/10, since I don't know yet if it stays as fun after several plays.

**7th Seal** (*designed by Julio Falagán, published by Devir*): I fell in love with this solo game, where you break the seven seals and fight bosses and minions with dice. I only played one round, but it was enough to enjoy it: the art is really nice, and so is the gameplay. I've pre-ordered it too, so I'll test it more once it arrives.

**The Fog: Escape from Paradise** (*designed by Robert Müller-Reinwarth, published by XOLLOX Games*): a competitive game where you get your people to the boats before a deadly fog catches them. I played a short demo version at three players. My first impression was that you never have enough moves to save your people, or enough of them, but I suppose that's the whole point: who will you sacrifice, how close to the beach will they get, and who will you save? The closer to the beach someone is when the fog catches them, the fewer points you lose, so you need to find a balance. I'd love to play a full game to form a better opinion, but so far it's a hit for me.

## Other Games Played

### Dune: Imperium

*Designed by Paul Dennen, published by Dire Wolf.*

Dune: Imperium puts you at the head of one of the great houses of the Dune universe, mixing worker placement, deck building and influence majority. The first player to reach 10 points triggers the end of the game.

I'll confess its dark, strange look made me hesitate for a long time. But the game shines solo, with a deck of cards or a companion app running your two rivals, and it's probably even better multiplayer. My first game, as Paul Atreides, ended in an 11 to 9 win... with an asterisk, since I played the trash rule wrong in my favour. The next two brought me back down to earth: 6 to 10 with Helena Richese, then 5 to 11 with Duke Leto Atreides. It still earns an 8/10 from me.

### Planta Nubo

*Designed by Michael Keller, Andreas Odendahl and Uwe Rosenberg, published by The Game Builders.*

In Planta Nubo, you play in a future world where you take care of the Earth, creating your own plantations and forests to deliver flowers. It's a moderately complex game with lots of small rules. The Botbot is an automa with different difficulty levels: it can be hard to manage at first, but it gets easier after a few turns. I've only played once, and I won 94 to 63! I loved it. The components are beautiful, and I love the idea of delivering flowers in so many colours. It gets a 7.5/10, and it will definitely be back on my table next month.

### Quick Notes

**Castellion** (*designed by Shadi Torbey, published by inPatience*): you draw tiles one by one to build your castle, using their powers along the way, and at the end of each ordeal your towers and bastions are put to the test. I've played it several times hoping it would click, and it hasn't yet. I enjoy building with the tiles, and the components are good quality, but losing without any clue how I could have won is frustrating. If I find the right strategy, I'll probably love it. It's a 6/10 today, but I'm not giving up.

**Codex Naturalis** (*designed by Thomas Dupont, published by Bombyx*): you continue the work of a monk finishing his book about nature, and if you've followed my [posts about the art in my games](/en/posts/20260927-board-game-art-part-1/), you know how much I love the gold on its cards. This month I tried the solo mode for the first time, after discovering it in a video by [Unna](https://www.youtube.com/@unna), a Spanish reviewer I really appreciate. You play against an automa that takes cards from the same river as you, and I lost 30 to 34. It's still fun solo, though I need more plays to form a real opinion, and in multiplayer it's great too. I'm giving it 7.5/10.

## Final Thoughts

Even though I've been working a lot and had less time to play, I'm really happy with my October. I learnt some new games, and above all, I got to go to SPIEL Essen for the first time. It was a wonderful experience, and I'll definitely be back next year.

The game that stood out this month is Moustache: such a simple game that made me laugh so much. Its lovely illustrations make it even better.

## Looking Ahead

For November, I'd like to play more quick games, like Gloomhaven: Buttons & Bugs and Conservas. As for bigger games, maybe Kingdom Rush: Elemental Uprising, which I'm really eager to try.
