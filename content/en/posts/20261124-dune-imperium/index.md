## Game Overview

Dune: Imperium is a 2020 release for 1–4 players, ages 14+, with a playtime of 60–120 minutes. It was designed by Paul Dennen, illustrated by Clay Brooks, Derek Herring, Kenan Jackson, Brett Nienburg, Raul Ramos and Nate Storm, and published by Dire Wolf. I own the Spanish edition, also published by Dire Wolf.

It won the 2020 Golden Geek award for Best Card Game and was nominated for the 2022 Kennerspiel des Jahres.

## Theme

The game is based on Frank Herbert's 1965 novel Dune. You play one of the leaders of the Dune universe and try to lead your house to victory. To get there, you'll need to beat your rivals in combat, play the political factions shrewdly and get your hands on precious cards.

## How to Win

The first player to reach 10 victory points triggers the end of the game. The game also ends if the conflict deck runs out. Either way, the player with the most points wins.

## Component Quality

The components are okay overall, but the player boards are too thin. The aesthetic isn't to my taste. I understand why it's so dark, since it reflects the harshness of Arrakis, but it still looks dull to me. That's only my personal opinion, and it's one of the reasons it took me so long to try this game.

## Gameplay

Dune: Imperium mixes worker placement, deck building and card-driven combat.

Every player starts with the same deck, but each leader has a different power, which you activate by playing your Signet Ring card. You begin with two agents (your workers) and can gain a third later in the game.

On your turn, you play a card for its location icons, place an agent on one of those locations and resolve its action. If the card has an effect in its top section, you resolve that too.

Many locations belong to one of four factions: the Emperor, the Spacing Guild, the Bene Gesserit and the Fremen. Visiting them raises your influence with that faction. Enough influence earns you victory points, and the player with the most influence gains the faction's alliance and an extra point, which a rival can take from you later.

Along the way you also collect Intrigue cards. These secret cards can boost your strength in combat, give you a plot effect on your turn or reward you at the end of the game.

Each round has a conflict card showing what the first, second and third place players will win. Once all your agents are placed, the cards left in your hand come into play. They add strength to the troops you sent into that round's conflict, and they give you persuasion to buy new cards for your deck. Some cards also let you trash a card, which removes it from your deck for good.

## Solo Mode

In solo you face two rivals, chosen at random from the pool of leaders, and their actions are driven by a deck of cards. They're simple to run, but it's easy to forget to deploy their troops, since only a small combat symbol reminds you. And don't underestimate them: as my scores below show, they are no pushovers.

I usually don't like apps taking part in a game, but here I recommend the Dire Wolf Game Room app. Its assistant manages your rivals and gives you a checklist for each round. I played my first game without it, and using it in the next two helped a lot.

## My Experience

I played Dune: Imperium solo three times in October and really enjoyed it.

- **Game 1:** I played Paul Atreides against Count Memnon Thorvald and Glossu "The Beast" Rabban, and won with 11 points. Memnon finished with 9 and Rabban with 7. To be honest, I got the trashing rule wrong: instead of trashing a card, I only discarded it, so it stayed in my deck. That went in my favour.
- **Game 2:** As Helena Richese, I finished with only 6 points. Count Memnon Thorvald and Duke Leto Atreides both reached 10, and Memnon took the win.
- **Game 3:** As Duke Leto, it was even harder. I scored 5, while Glossu Rabban won with 11 and Countess Ariana Thorvald reached 10.

After my first win I thought it would always be that easy. It wasn't! I'm humble enough to admit this game is hard to win.

What I liked most is how every card has more than one use. You need a good balance of locations, but you also want to use your leader's power to your advantage and keep enough strength for combat. That balance is the whole beauty of the game.

The combat is really interesting too. Each round you look at the rewards and decide whether they're worth the effort, or whether you'd be better off focusing somewhere else.

{{< figure
  src="dune-imperium-game-2.jpg"

There's a real race to build the best deck, and it plays differently from other deckbuilders. In most of them you build up your deck generously, but here you don't want to buy too many cards. A small deck can be a blessing, because you draw the cards you want most more often.

## My Rating

I give Dune: Imperium an 8/10: I loved the gameplay, the visuals much less so.

## Final Thoughts

The gameplay is well worth trying, and I can clearly understand the hype around this game. I've only played it solo so far. It plays well that way, but because of the combat I think it really shines with more players. Solo, it wouldn't be my first choice, but it was a real pleasure to discover it.
